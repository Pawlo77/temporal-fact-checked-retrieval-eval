# StreamingQA

> [!info] TL;DR
> StreamingQA gives every question a question date and every news article a publication date, so QA models can be evaluated quarterly over 2020 while only articles published up to that quarter are searchable. Adding new articles to the retrieval index gives open-book models fast adaptation with almost no forgetting, but models whose language model is stale still trail those with an updated or retrained one. Dated questions also make a dense retriever far better at finding recent evidence.

## Motivation

- QA had been evaluated on static snapshots such as Wikipedia; temporal QA datasets dated facts or documents but not the questions ([Dhingra et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00459)), or dated questions over a static corpus.
- Language models degrade on text from after their training period ([[Mind the Gap]]); whether that degradation reaches downstream QA, and whether retrieval offsets it, was untested.
- Real users ask about both last week and years ago, so a benchmark needs both recent and past questions to measure adaptation and forgetting.

## Method

Notation of this vault, with QA terms: question $q$ asked at $t_q$; document $f$ published at $t_f$.

**Task.** Questions are grouped into four quarters of 2020 by $t_q$. A question in quarter $Q$ is answered from the corpus of articles with $t_f$ up to the end of $Q$, so the searchable pool grows each quarter.

Intuition: the pool a question may use is the pool that existed when it was asked, at quarter granularity.

**Recent and past subsets.** Recent questions are about articles from the month before $t_q$; past questions about articles from any time between 2007 and $t_q$, with absolute or relative time phrases added ("3 months ago").

**Questions.** Generated by few-shot prompting a large language model on an article and an answer span, or written by annotators; all filtered by three annotators for quality and answered twice more for extra references.

**Models.** Closed-book: a 448M-parameter Transformer-XL trained to end of 2019 (stale), fine-tuned month by month on 2020 news, or retrained to end of 2020. Open-book: RAG-style model with DPR retrieving 20 six-sentence passages, and Fusion-in-Decoder (T5) with 64 passages. Dates are prefixed to questions and articles.

**Lag analysis.** Scores are grouped by the lag between the end of the model's knowledge and the question quarter: negative lag means missing knowledge, positive lag means possible forgetting.

## Key numbers

- Dated DPR: median gap between retrieved and gold article dates is 41 days with dates vs 1,101 days without; recall@20 on recent generated questions 57% vs 43%.
- Fine-tuning on one month of news needs 95% fewer steps than full retraining.
- Closed-book F1 is about 10 points higher for questions with frequent named entities; DPR recall@1 drops about 5 points as entity frequency rises.
- FiD F1 on past generated questions: 0.711 with no time phrase, 0.469 with an absolute phrase, 0.359 with a relative phrase.

## Experiments

- **Closed-book adaptation:** monthly fine-tuning improves recent-subset scores without catastrophic forgetting; on past questions it drifts about 5% below the stale model at long positive lag; retraining remains best.
- **Open-book adaptation:** adding articles to the index gives a steep gain from lag -1Q to 0Q for all variants, with almost no forgetting; updating or retraining the generator adds further gains.
- **Parametric vs semi-parametric:** retrieval helps most for rare entities, parametric updates for frequent ones, where dense retrieval is confused by redundancy.
- **Static vs updated knowledge:** open-book models do worse on answers that may change (FiD 0.435 vs 0.494 F1).

## Data

- Knowledge corpus: about 11M English WMT news articles, 2007-2020, split into 42.1M (to 2019) and 47.6M (to 2020) passages.
- Questions: 99,402 train and 9,939 validation (generated, 2007-2019); 27,620 generated and 8,758 written evaluation questions (2020).

> [!failure] Limitations
> - Quarter granularity: within a quarter, a question can retrieve articles published after it.
> - Retrieval training uses a restricted search space holding the gold evidence, not the full pool.
> - T5 and BERT were pretrained before 2020 but on uncontrolled data, so their prior knowledge is less precisely bounded than Transformer-XL's.
> - Conflicting answers from updated facts are left out of scope; English news only.

## Relation to our project

- The closest design to our rolling-origin protocol: dated queries over a dated, growing pool, evaluated per period ([[Proposed Task]]). Our causal pool $P(q)$ is stricter, per query rather than per quarter.
- It separates index freshness from model freshness, which maps onto our split between index leakage and parametric leakage in LLM rerankers ([[Enhanced topic research]]).
- The lag analysis is a template for our time-to-findability curve: performance as a function of the gap between query and knowledge.
- Dated retrieval helped DPR; for PFCR a date feature would itself use $t_f$, so any such system must respect the causal pool ([[Chronological Evaluation]]).

## Future work

- **Partly done:** questions about knowledge that changes over time, evaluated weekly on fresh news; [Kasai et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13332).
- **Partly done:** search-augmented LLMs on fast-changing and false-premise questions; [Vu et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03214).
- **Open:** closing the gap between stale and retrained generators with continual-learning methods, as the authors ask (searched 2026-10).
- **Open:** a per-query causal pool rather than per-quarter snapshots in streaming retrieval benchmarks (searched 2026-10).
